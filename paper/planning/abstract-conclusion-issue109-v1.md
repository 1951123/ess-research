## Proposed title

*Extended Statistics Selection under Estimator Strategy Uncertainty*

## Abstract

Choosing which extended statistics to maintain is a resource-allocation problem, but an installed set need not determine which overlapping statistics a cardinality estimator will apply. An advisor that knows the available objects but not the estimator's downstream application strategy may therefore evaluate the same resource set under different complete estimation schemes. We formulate **extended-statistics selection under estimator-strategy uncertainty**, separating the unordered resource decision, task-specific admissible scheme families, estimation loss, and maintenance budget. We distinguish fixed-priority Nominal selection from task-wise pessimistic (PESS) selection and from risk induced by a single global priority shared across tasks. For each fixed resource set, the expected global-priority loss is bounded by the worst shared-global-priority loss, which is bounded by the task-wise pessimistic loss, under common model and admissibility assumptions. A labeled maximal-matching instantiation connects this distinction to a restricted PostgreSQL 16.14 two-column MCV selection path.

We evaluate frozen Train-selected Nominal and PESS resources on four AreCEL workloads, combining exact offline priority-model analysis, a real Train-task scheme-space case study, and controlled held-out PostgreSQL `Plan Rows` measurements. In canonical fixed-order configurations at budget five, Nominal achieves lower Test P95 Q-error on all four datasets. In a separate **sampled counterfactual uniform global relative-OID-order evaluation**, PESS has lower estimated expected per-order Test P95 on Census13 and DMV11, Nominal has lower expected P95 on Forest10, and the Power7 contrast is inconclusive within the permutation-sampling interval. The distribution is an evaluation assumption, not a claim about deployed OID randomness. These results show that the assumed estimator application strategy can change the resources favored by an advisor, without establishing universally better pessimistic selections or physical worst-case guarantees.

## 9. Conclusion

This paper examined an information gap in extended-statistics selection: an advisor controls **which statistics are installed**, but may not control or reliably know **which admissible combination of those statistics an estimator will apply** to a particular cardinality-estimation task. Overlapping resources make this gap concrete. A fixed unordered resource set can support several complete application schemes with different modeled estimation losses. Budgeted selection should therefore state not only a resource and maintenance model, but also the assumptions under which the estimator will use the selected resources.

We formalized this distinction through task-indexed admissible scheme families \(\Gamma_i(M)\), with the installed set \(M\) as the advisor's sole decision variable. Fixed-priority Nominal, shared-global-policy risk, and task-wise pessimistic PESS encode different kinds of strategy knowledge. For a given \(M\) and compatible scheme/loss definitions, uniform global-priority expected loss \(A(M)\), worst shared-global-priority loss \(G(M)\), and task-wise pessimistic loss \(P(M)\) satisfy \(A(M)\leq G(M)\leq P(M)\). The second inequality can be strict when task-specific worst schemes cannot be realized simultaneously by one priority order shared across tasks. These statements are **fixed-set properties of the prescribed model**, not cross-selection rankings or unconditional guarantees on real PostgreSQL estimates.

The structured two-column MCV instance makes the modeling mechanism inspectable. Eligible labeled resources form a graph, and its inclusion-maximal matchings represent complete admissible applications under stated conditions. The frozen Census13 Train case study exhibits a real task for which adding overlapping resources yields three alternative modeled schemes with distinct absolute-log losses. Across the B5 Nominal Census13 Train collection, 26,998 tasks admit multiple modeled schemes, but only 10,764 have positive Nominal-to-PESS risk; ambiguity itself does not imply deterioration. Separately, version-pinned PostgreSQL 16.14 source reasoning and controlled object-order experiments reveal a restricted path where deterministic catalog priority affects applied MCV objects and resulting physical cardinality estimates. The restricted correspondence does not equate all PostgreSQL behavior with matching semantics.

The empirical comparisons highlight why the policy assumption matters. The frozen Train model and exact priority enumeration characterize different risks for already-selected Nominal and PESS sets without claiming an optimal outer search; both sets were produced by the same add-only greedy heuristic. Under one canonical physical order, Nominal has lower held-out Test P95 Q-error at B5 in all four workloads. Under the distinct, deliberately uniform *global-order* counterfactual evaluated with a frozen 156-configuration physical sampling design, the estimated expected per-order Test P95 comparison favors PESS on Census13 and DMV11 and Nominal on Forest10, while Power7 is inconclusive under the stated permutation-only intervals. None of these contrasts establishes that real PostgreSQL samples OID orders uniformly or that model-level worst log loss bounds physical Test Q-error.

The principal contribution is therefore a **decision-level formulation** of uncertainty about the estimator's downstream application strategy, coupled with explicit objective relationships and scoped systems evidence. Prior work already studies statistics selection, interacting statistical resources, alternative estimator decompositions, and uncertainty-aware recommendations; we do not claim to originate those ideas. The narrower distinction made here is the **resource-dependent complete-scheme family together with an advisor's incomplete knowledge of subsequent scheme choice**. Future work could develop empirically grounded policy families, richer estimators and overlapping-resource structures, methods for identifying when taskwise conservatism is unnecessarily strong, and selection procedures with stronger algorithmic guarantees. Such extensions would also require evaluating maintenance overhead and downstream plan quality directly. Within the limits of this study, estimator application strategy is a consequential part of the **resource-selection problem**, rather than an implementation detail that an advisor can always ignore.
