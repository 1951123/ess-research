# Section 7 — Threats to Validity (Issue #109, v1)

**Status:** standalone English manuscript draft for review, following merged Sections 1–6. It distinguishes formal assumptions, implementation correspondence, empirical construct validity, and external validity. This document proposes no new experiments and does not replace `paper/main.tex`.

## 7. Threats to Validity

Our central claim is that estimator application strategy is an optimization-relevant dimension of extended-statistics selection. Its validity rests on different kinds of evidence: a formally prescribed model, a conditional source-level correspondence for PostgreSQL 16.14, offline computations on frozen Train models, and physical evaluations on held-out query workloads. We distinguish limitations of each layer instead of treating agreement at one layer as validation of all others.

### 7.1 Model coverage and construct validity

**Prescribed schemes versus actual behavior.** The family \(\Gamma_i(M)\) is a nonempty model of *complete* admissible estimation schemes. It is not defined by observing every behavior of a deployed estimator. For a physical scheme family \(\mathcal R_i(M)\), containment \(\mathcal R_i(M)\subseteq\Gamma_i(M)\) has not been established generally. Therefore the modeled task-wise worst loss \(P(M)\) is **not an unconditional bound** on PostgreSQL errors. Even when containment holds, a physical bound further requires that the model estimate and the evaluated physical estimate induce the same loss for corresponding schemes. Neither requirement follows from a matching theorem alone.

**Complete-scheme granularity.** Modeling a scheme as a maximal matching is useful when overlapping two-column resources contend for single-attribute clauses. A deployed cardinality estimator may use mixed statistics kinds, fallback independence factors, distinct estimation stages, joins, OR clauses, expression statistics, duplicate-attribute predicates or other internal state. Some of these mechanisms cannot be encoded as a simple set of nonoverlapping edges. General model notation permits richer \(\Omega_i\) and \(\Gamma_i\); the present experiments instantiate only the restricted pairwise model. We do not conclude that all physical scheme families are nonnested, exhaustive, or matching-based.

**Loss and maintenance proxies.** The frozen training model uses a positive, clamped-log-derived multiplicative correction approximation and *pre-output* absolute-log loss evaluated with exact rational arithmetic. The original `census13-clamped-log-v2` preparation has specific normalization and quantization conventions; it should not be conflated with raw unprocessed cardinalities or PostgreSQL planner output. The generic optimization framework allows an arbitrary maintenance function, but the evaluated implementation uses a **unit/additive per-object budget proxy**. No experiment directly measured ANALYZE CPU consumption over a maintenance horizon, production write overhead or actual storage/refresh trade-offs. Consequently, conclusions concern accuracy-related modeling under this proxy, not realized resource economic efficiency.

**Nonmonotonicity and representative mechanisms.** Abstract maximal-matching families are not necessarily nested when \(M\) grows; this is a mathematical property, not a measured frequency in the studied workloads. The real Census13 Train witness shows one, two and three alternative maximal schemes under nested Nominal B3–B5 additions **without an old scheme disappearing**. B5 Nominal versus PESS uses different sets and must not be interpreted as a nested-set intervention. The deterministic witness is selected to reveal a mechanism, not to estimate its prevalence by random sampling or establish that one task determined the whole-workload resource choice. Of the 26,998 ambiguous B5 Nominal Census13 Train tasks, 16,234 have zero modeled risk gap; ambiguity alone does not imply error regression.

### 7.2 Internal validity of the PostgreSQL correspondence

Our maximal-matching correspondence is **conditional on A1–A4**: compatible simple single-relation AND predicates with distinct relevant attributes, built two-column MCV objects, no upstream clause consumption/interference, and tied greedy ranking that resolves by a fixed candidate-list priority. The proof establishes a property of selected objects under those sufficient preconditions. It does not prove equivalence of the full physical cardinality-estimation formula to the frozen multiplicative model.

Source inspection is pinned to PostgreSQL **16.14** and a specific source path. A statistics-object OID-sorted list can induce deterministic tie priority, but a fixed catalog is not probabilistic. The early AB–BC–CD trace used instrumented DEBUG1 diagnostics on a project-specific development build and only two constructed orders. Recreating objects in that fixture can affect samples, so the fixture alone is not a tightly content-controlled causal accuracy comparison. The subsequent #105/#107 physical measurements controlled fixed-sample replay, ordinary and MCV-content fingerprints and source/version/settings to sharpen their order comparisons. These are strong within-setup checks, not a proof of arbitrary-order realizability or behavior for unseen SQL structures and PostgreSQL versions.

A global order shared by multiple tasks need not permit independently chosen task-wise worst schemes. Conversely, exact *single-task* realization of each maximal matching under **freely varied abstract priorities** does not establish that every desired order can be created in a particular production catalog without incidental state changes. We therefore avoid interpreting abstract \(A(M)\), \(G(M)\) or \(P(M)\) as the directly observed distribution of real deployments.

### 7.3 Selection and inference validity

**Heuristic resource optimization.** Both Nominal and PESS choices come from the same deterministic, strictly improving, add-only greedy algorithm on Train. No swap, deletion, multiple-start or exact outer combinatorial optimization was performed. Thus observed differences demonstrate the behavior of the **chosen objectives through this frozen heuristic**, not necessarily the globally optimal resource sets for those objectives. The budget is a cap; resource count can be smaller than \(B\).

**Offline permutation results versus physical observations.** #103 exactly enumerates global priority orders for **25 fixed selected configurations** (906 permutations total) using the **frozen Train-model absolute-log total loss**, not physical Test estimates. It gives exact values within that model but cannot validate modeled cardinality effects out of sample. Model-internal inequalities \(A(M)\leq G(M)\leq P(M)\) apply to one fixed \(M\), compatible induced schemes and one common loss; they cannot rank separately selected sets. The observed B5 \(P(M)-G(M)\) gap is positive on all four Nominal selected sets and zero on the four PESS selected sets in this frozen analysis. Neither phenomenon proves a general optimizer guarantee or the physical coverage of \(\Gamma_i(M)\).

**Physical outcomes and comparisons.** #101 evaluates official held-out Test queries at a fixed configuration/order and finds that B5 Nominal has lower observed P95 than PESS for all four datasets. #105 intervenes on exactly canonical versus reverse creation orders of four Nominal B5 sets, not every possible priority. #107 evaluates a different statistic: *expected across global relative-OID orders of per-order held-out Test P95*, with a deliberately uniform order distribution. Its preference for PESS in Census13 and DMV11 and Nominal in Forest10 is not a logical contradiction of the #101 canonical-order values, because the target policy distributions differ. Power7's estimated contrast interval includes zero. The results cannot identify the isolated causal effect of an objective holding resource membership fixed: Nominal and PESS may install different objects and even different counts.

**Order-population sampling.** #107 completes 156 physical arms, not a full enumeration of the 732 order/selected-set combinations. Four five-object Nominal sets use two fixed certainty orders plus simple random samples of 22 of the 118 residual orders; two five-object PESS sets use 24 samples of 120; two three-object PESS sets are exact six-order censuses. Design weights and finite-population corrections are required. Approximate t intervals describe **permutation-sampling variability conditional on the fixed Test collection** only; they do not represent sampling uncertainty from new queries, datasets, restored statistics, table snapshots, or PostgreSQL versions. Degenerate sample variance for a 24/120 five-object PESS set is not proof that the remaining 96 orders are invariant. Sample extrema do not certify global best/worst orders.

**Protocol chronology and execution exceptions.** The #107 sampling protocol was established after inspecting prior #101/#105 summaries; while sampled order IDs were drawn without using the new Test outcomes and then frozen, the entire analysis plan should not be described as independent of earlier evidence. The eight previously measured certainty arms failed a reuse-eligibility check labeled raw-observation identity/recomputation, whose exact row-level cause was not preserved; they were remeasured at their predeclared orders. In the initial #107 run, 12 three-object PESS arms failed a catalog-size precheck **before** EXPLAIN; an authorized continuation completed those same prespecified orders. Neither incident justifies guessing a root cause or saying all runs succeeded on their first attempt. Raw physical observations and run manifests are retained externally with documented hashes, rather than wholly embedded in Git.

### 7.4 Metric interpretation and generalization

**Training objective versus planner-output Q-error.** The structured optimization uses a task-specific pre-clamp log-loss model, whereas physical Test uses PostgreSQL top-level `Plan Rows` and the benchmark's zero-aware Q-error after planner rounding/clamping. Percentile Q-error is nonlinear in the query error distribution, and the #107 primary estimand computes the P95 **within each order before averaging over orders**. Hence the offline expected *total* log loss (#103) and expected *per-order P95* physical Q-error (#107) differ in loss, aggregation, train/test split and estimator mechanism. Agreement or disagreement of resource rankings between them is **not on its own a calibration, consistency or generalization test** for the modeled objective.

**Workload and environment scope.** The four original AreCEL Train/Test workloads are fixed evaluation splits, not random representatives of all query workloads. They emphasize the selected single-table predicate structures and pairwise MCV resources. Their public benchmark environment and statistics targets are not exactly replicated; our held-out measurement uses a pinned instrumented PostgreSQL 16.14 build and target 300. Results may change with stronger correlations, different overlap graphs, predicate types, joins, an alternative DBMS, other statistics kinds, different ANALYZE samples, or database updates. Reported P50/P95/P99 and maximum are accuracy proxies; there is no measured query latency, plan-cost optimality or end-to-end advisor deployment improvement.

**Distributional meaning of strategy uncertainty.** An inaccessible deterministic choice rule can motivate advisor uncertainty without any random system behavior. Uniform global relative-OID permutations in #103 and #107 are **analytical and counterfactual evaluation assumptions**, not a fitted posterior over real catalog histories or empirical OID frequencies. Priorities act globally across tasks rather than as independent per-query scheme draws. Robustness conclusions must specify the family of policies and the loss being measured, not silently substitute a task-independent worst-case interpretation for a shared policy.

### 7.5 Scope of supported conclusions

The supported conclusion is specific: **under an explicit resource-and-scheme model, estimator application strategy can change the evaluated risk and the resource sets selected by an advisor**. The frozen Train scheme-space witness and exact permutation analysis demonstrate this modeling mechanism. A restricted PostgreSQL 16.14 path and physically controlled OID-order comparisons establish its plausible systems relevance. The held-out physical results show dataset- and policy-assumption-dependent trade-offs, not a universal advantage for task-wise pessimistic resource selection. Stronger claims about physical worst-case bounds, other statistical estimators, actual deployment distributions, globally optimal sets, maintenance cost or execution latency would require additional assumptions and independent evidence.
